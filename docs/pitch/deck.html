01 · The problem

The spending limit on your AI agent is enforced by the same software you're protecting yourself from.

Teams are putting tool-calling agents in front of real money. The caps live in config and a check before submitting, inside the process the agent runs in. Four ordinary failures all end in the same place:

Prompt injection

A bad tool call

A compromised dependency

An ordinary bug

→ a wallet that does what it is told.

On a bad day, the loss is bounded by the balance, not by the policy.

Every other day, the automation nobody switches on, because the only safe setting is a human approving everything.

03 · The solution

The owner signs a policy. The ledger enforces it.

Output of sentry-agent request, an external program holding the agent's own credential.

RequestTransfer is the agent's only choice on the policy. Inside it, payments settle with nobody watching. Outside it, they wait for the owner. Over the balance, they are refused. Every outcome leaves a record.

04 · How Canton is used

Canton does the enforcing, the hiding and the sharing.

Daml is the enforcement

Caps, allowlist, rolling window and escalation are checked inside RequestTransfer. No client-side check to patch around.

Sub-transaction privacy

The policy is signed by the owner, observed by the agent, and delivered to no counterparty's node.

Three participants

One synchronizer, one transaction settling across nodes, each node asked what it holds.

Shared control

Releasing or refusing a held request needs 2 of 2 confirmations, through BitSafe's Decentralization Manager.

No single host

The owner is hosted on two participants at threshold 1, and kept spending through a host outage.

CIP-0103 wallets

Discovered through Canton's dApp SDK. The wallet submits as its own party, no actAs.

IssueBank mints a holding
DelegateOwner signs the policy
RequestAgent asks to pay
DecideSettle, hold or refuse
RecordEvery outcome on ledger

06 · Business brief

For teams that already let software spend, and can't publish their limits.

SegmentDigital-asset treasury and operations teams, 10 to 500 people, already running unattended payment automation. First on Canton: validator operators, tokenized fund administrators, wallet and payments teams.
UserThe platform engineer who operates the agent, holds its credential and knows their own if statement is the last line of defence.
BuyerWhoever answers for the money: head of treasury, COO, or the founder.
Who paysFor the operational layer: hosted participants, approvals, policy management across agents, reporting. Per agent per month (hypothesis). The contracts stay Apache-2.0 and free, because anyone must be able to check them.
Why CantonThe only ledger where the limit is enforced by something the agent can't reach and still hidden from everyone but the owner and the agent.
Not forRetail users, consumer assistants spending API credit, and teams happy with public caps: Spend Permissions already serves them.

07 · Go-to-market and pilot plan

Distribute through wallets, prove it with a refusal.

Channels
  • The probe as the artefact. A refusal an engineer ran is pasted into their team's chat. A dashboard is demoed once.
  • CIP-0103 wallets. Nocturnal and Grofty give users a party and a key but no bounded delegation. We are the missing half, they have the users.
  • Canton ecosystem, directly. Validators and fund administrators are a small, published list: ten conversations.
  • Open contracts. Adoption of the Daml is worth more than signups.
Pilot · three steps · 90 days
1

A real wallet signs. An owner signs a policy from a CIP-0103 wallet we did not write.

Integration: @canton-network/dapp-sdk, already wired

2

A real asset moves. The holding becomes a token-standard asset instead of demo units.

Integration: the Canton token standard

3

A real agent runs for a month. One design partner, one agent, one real cap, on a validator that hosts the owner.

Integration: the partner's participant and their agent's credential. Success is not volume: the held queue caught what it should, and every outcome can be explained from the record.

09 · What is not built, and what we ask for

Claim narrowly. Then ask for the three things that decide it.

Not built yet
  • The asset is self-contained, not a token standard
  • The issuer mints on request, like a faucet
  • LocalNet only, participants run in memory
  • No real wallet extension has signed yet
  • One person runs all three nodes, so the operators aren't independent
The ask · not funding
1

Ten introductions to people accountable for an agent that already spends.

2

A MainNet validator path, so a real wallet signs end to end.

3

A token standard to sit on, so a pilot moves real value.
