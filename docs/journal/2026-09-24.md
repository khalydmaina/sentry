# 24 September 2026

**Stage: Metrics.**

Spent today turning claims into measurements, because the materials had drifted
ahead of the code. The metrics and demo documents still described a two-node
network, and the network has had three participants since yesterday afternoon.
Rather than edit the numbers to match, I ran everything again on a live ledger
and wrote down what actually came back.

What the run produced: 31 Daml scripts passing, 25 of them tests. The mutation
suite re-run and still catching 7 of 7 deliberate contract errors, with the
contract restored clean. The agent probe refused 4 of 4, balance unchanged at
960.00 either side. Four requests giving four different verdicts: 40 executed,
120 held on the per-transaction cap, 10 held on the allowlist, 5000 rejected on
balance. Shared control refused a release at one confirmation and settled it at
two, with the confirmations coming from parties on different participants. The
owner survived losing a host and getting it back.

The privacy claim got sharper rather than just larger. It is no longer "the
counterparty cannot see the policy". It is: put the query to the counterparty's
participant **as the owner's own party** and it still returns nothing, because
the contract was never delivered to that node. A per-party filter has nothing to
do there. That is the version worth defending.

Measuring found two real bugs, which is the argument for measuring. The privacy
page had no row for the governance library's execution receipt, which is the
only governance contract still active after a release, so a page whose whole job
is showing what each node holds was dropping one. And its banner still claimed
the policy never leaves the wallet node, which quietly became false the moment
the owner gained a second host. Both fixed and verified in a browser.

I also corrected a line in the demo material that said Grofty access "was
requested during the hackathon". It was not. Rewritten to say an end-to-end
Grofty demo depends on access we do not hold.

**What I got wrong today.** I spent a while designing a way to ship captured
ledger responses with the hosted site so judges could click through a demo with
no ledger behind it. Real data, clearly labelled as a recording. It is still
hardcoding, and this project's entire argument is that you should not trust a
claim you cannot check. Wiring canned responses through the live UI cuts against
that even with a banner on it. Dropped it. The hosted page will keep saying
plainly that the ledger runs locally, and the demo will be a recording that
nobody can mistake for a live system.

**Success metric for the demo.** We will know this works when three engineers
who are not me clone the repo, run `sentry-agent probe` on their own machine, and
get 4 of 4 refused without asking me for help. Currently 0 of 3.

**Still missing, and it is not the code.** Zero user conversations. The project
page does not exist yet. The hosted site is behind a login. Those are the three
things standing between a working, measured MVP and a judged one.

**Next:** publish the site, create the project entry, and get the first of those
three conversations.
